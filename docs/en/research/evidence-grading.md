# Evidence Grading

Information on racing pigeon genetics and health comes from a wide range of sources, and its evidential strength is uneven. From rigorous peer-reviewed studies to scattered fancier experience, credibility differs enormously. Understanding an evidence grading system is the basis for reading information scientifically and deciding rationally.

## Plain-language explanation (an analogy)

Evidence grading is like **giving a piece of information a credit rating** - not every claim is equally trustworthy. A rigorous peer-reviewed study is like a "bank certificate of deposit"; experience passed on by word of mouth among fanciers is like "hearsay". Learning to read the "rating" is what lets you pick out the genuinely sound conclusions from a mass of information.

## Why evidence grading is needed

The racing pigeon field contains a great deal of "experiential" and "legendary" information, among which:

- some information has no scientific basis;
- some information is individual experience that has never been systematically verified;
- some information is exaggerated or misleading;
- conclusions that have actually been verified by rigorous research are relatively limited.

Evidence grading helps us tell credible information apart within a mass of claims and reach more rational judgements.

## The evidence level system

A simplified evidence grading framework (from high to low):

1. **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses**: combine several high-quality studies; the strongest evidence;
2. **Randomised controlled trials**: control variables and set up controls; stronger causal inference;
3. **Cohort and case-control studies**: observational studies that can suggest associations;
4. **Cross-sectional studies and case reports**: provide clues, with limited evidential strength;
5. **Expert opinion and experience**: has reference value but lacks systematic verification.

## Association versus causation

A key concept when reading genetic association studies is that **association is not causation**:

- **Association**: a genotype and a trait occur together statistically;
- **Causation**: the genotype directly causes the change in the trait;
- many gene association studies can only suggest a "possible association" and cannot establish causation;
- confounding factors, multiple testing and insufficient sample size can all produce spurious associations.

## Principles for reading research conclusions

When faced with research conclusions about racing pigeons, follow these principles:

1. **Check the source**: does it come from a peer-reviewed academic journal;
2. **Check the method**: is the study design rigorous and the sample size adequate;
3. **Check the wording**: is the conclusion cautious, does it acknowledge its limitations;
4. **Check reproducibility**: has the conclusion been verified repeatedly by more than one study;
5. **Stay cautious**: be wary of wording such as "absolute", "guaranteed" or "certain".

## This site's position on evidence

This research center holds to the following positions:

- cite only real, traceable literature;
- use cautious wording for research conclusions (such as "research suggests", "may be associated");
- keep "scientific evidence" and "experiential reference" clearly apart;
- do not exaggerate, do not fabricate, do not mislead.

# Genotype Profile and Racing Performance

**Published title**: Prospective study relating genotype profiles with race performance in racing pigeons

**Authors**: Geert Kolvenbag et al. (2022)

**Journal**: Journal of Applied Genetics

**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1007/s13353-022-00697-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13353-022-00697-w)

**PubMed**: [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35505002/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35505002/)

## Plain-language summary (an analogy)

This study is like **a controlled comparison of genes and results** - it set several genotype profiles next to actual race results to see how much genes **score**. The conclusion matters: the genetic contribution is **probabilistic**, like the advantage height gives a basketball player - it helps, but it does not decide who wins.

## Abstract

This prospective study related several genotype profiles (including DRD4 and LDHA) to race performance in racing pigeons and analysed the genetic contribution to performance tendency. The results suggest that the genetic contribution to performance is probabilistic rather than decisive.

## Research question

Is there an association between multiple genotype profiles and race performance in racing pigeons?

## Background

Flight performance in racing pigeons is a complex quantitative trait, and several candidate genes (such as DRD4 and LDHA) have been proposed as being associated with performance. A prospective design allows the gene-performance association to be assessed more reliably.

## Methods

A prospective study design was used: genotype data and race results were collected for racing pigeons, and the statistical association between genotype profiles and performance was analysed.

## Findings

The study found an association of some strength between several genotype profiles and race performance, but the association was limited in strength, suggesting that the genetic contribution is probabilistic.

## Interpretation of results

The research suggests that genes contribute to performance tendency in races, but this should not be read as a decisive effect. This is association evidence.

## Limitations

Differences in sample size, race conditions and the way performance is measured may affect how far the conclusions generalise.

## Significance

It supports the use of genetic testing as a reference tool in breeding, while stressing that it is probabilistic rather than deterministic.

## Evidence grade

**Association (association-level evidence)**

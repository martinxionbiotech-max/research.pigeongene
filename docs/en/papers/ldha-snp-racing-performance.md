# LDHA Polymorphism and Pigeon Survivability

**Published title**: LDHA gene is associated with pigeon survivability during racing competitions

**Authors**: Ramadan S, Miyake T, Yamaura J, Inoue-Murayama M (2018)

**Journal**: PLOS ONE

**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0195121](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0195121)

**PubMed**: [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29775483/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29775483/)

## Plain-language summary (an analogy)

The study is like **giving race entrants a genetic check-up** - it screens LDHA polymorphism and then looks at whether pigeons of different genotypes make it home from a race. The result points to a possible association, but it is an association and not causation - much like finding that people who train regularly are fitter, which does not mean that being fit is enough to win.

## Abstract

The study screened racing pigeons for LDHA polymorphism and analysed its association with racing survivability (whether the bird completed the race and returned home). The results showed a statistical association between LDHA genotype and racing survivability, suggesting that the gene may be associated with racing performance.

## Research question

Is there an association between LDHA polymorphism and racing survivability in racing pigeons?

## Background

LDHA is a key glycolytic enzyme involved in anaerobic energy metabolism. Racing places sustained pressure on a pigeon's metabolic capacity, and variation in metabolism-related genes may affect a bird's physiological adaptation and survival in mid-race.

## Methods

Samples were collected from racing pigeons, LDHA microsatellite polymorphism was screened, and genotypes were analysed for association with racing survival (whether the bird returned home successfully).

## Findings

A statistical association was found between LDHA genotype and racing survivability, suggesting that variation in this gene may be associated with racing performance.

## Interpretation of results

The study suggests an association between LDHA polymorphism and racing survivability. This is association-level evidence and does not amount to a causal conclusion.

## Limitations

An association study cannot prove causation; differences in sample background, race distance and race conditions may limit how far the conclusions can be generalised.

## Significance

It supports further research into LDHA as a candidate gene related to racing performance in pigeons.

## Evidence level

**Association (association-level evidence)**
